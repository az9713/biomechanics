M2 · B1K6, D5

Elastic similarity drawn as constant stress

K6 plotted a flat green line for bone stress across four decades of body mass. The module's own scaling does not give that. Under elastic similarity, diameter goes as length to the 3/2, so mass goes as length to the fourth and stress rises as the eighth root of body mass.

Wasσ constant, “near 50 MPa across four decades” — a regime the module never derives.
Now18.0 MPa at 0.02 kg → 85.3 MPa at 5000 kg. A factor 4.7 from shrew to elephant. Constant stress needs a third rule, d ∝ L².
M2 · B1D5 solution

“Similar bones bow to the same shape”

Same curvature is not the same shape. A beam of length L at curvature κ sags by κL², so the sag relative to its own length grows linearly with size.

Wasκ ∝ λ⁰, therefore the same shape at every scale.
Nowκ is scale-free but δ/L ∝ λ. Human to elephant, λ = 4.1: four times more sag for its length.
M1 · B1Problem D4

The elbow flexor pulled the wrong way

D4's muscle force pulled the forearm away from the joint. Its conclusion that the joint is in compression then followed from a sign error rather than from the mechanics.

What the gates could not see

All nine gates returned zero while these were still in the files. They were found by rendering each changed figure and looking at the image.

  • A wedge cut on the wrong diagonal. With the stated shear sense, the plane carrying positive shear runs top-left to bottom-right. The dashed cut ran the other way, and the principal stress arrow did not lie along the outward normal. Valid SVG throughout.
  • A legend printed through a number. K2's “69.7” sat under legend text. check_overlap compares text against curves and dashed reference lines — it does not test text against text.
  • A curve leaving the top of its axes. K4's τr = 7 trace ran past the frame; the y-axis now reaches 1.45.
  • An optimum marked half a degree off. K5's minimum was searched on a 0.5° grid, so the marker read 23.5° while the prose, caption, aria-label and the module's own code all said 23.6°. The true optimum is 23.578°.

Each gate tests one property. A wrong diagonal is valid SVG; a coarse grid is valid Python. The gates are necessary and not sufficient.

What already held

The mathematics was sound in both modules and none of it was touched. Module 1's Propositions and its section 8 worked example reproduce every figure they state — 18.9 N·m, 631 N, 567 N, 12.9×, 0.83 body weights — and all sixty-five numbers checked against its code matched. Module 2's Theorem 3.2, Proposition 3.3 and Proposition 4.1 each carry a proof of matching weight, and its eleven code blocks print exactly what the text claims. One mismatch in one hundred and twenty-nine numbers is a low rate; the failures were of statement, scaffolding and drawing, not of calculation.

Two additions went beyond the reports and are recorded in the commits. Module 2's D7 figure was a drawing of a Mohr's circle, and the report removes “Mohr's circle” from the text around it; leaving the figure would have orphaned it, so it became a wedge-balance diagram — which is where the wrong diagonal was found. Module 1's aria-label rewriter had been deleting \sin \beta from one label, leaving a screen reader to announce “horizontal moment arms grow as .”

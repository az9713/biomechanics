The four constraints that bind every future session

  1. Rigor parity. A boxed result is proved unless the box says why it cannot be. Module 0 has exactly two unproved boxes and both are labelled — Marko–Siggia (an interpolation) and the Hill equation (a fitted curve, left deliberately unboxed).
  2. Ask where every table value came from. This caught three errors in Module 0. A parameter that arrives looking derived may be fitted one step upstream: §2's Born exponent was fitted to alkali-halide compressibilities, §3's ΔG°′ is a fitted equilibrium model, §4's persistence length is fitted with the very model that consumes it.
  3. Do not assert a limit you have not computed. This is the module's own recurring defect and it recurred four times. Every sentence of the form “cannot exceed”, “at most” or “half the drop by” needs a line in a verification script behind it.
  4. Full gate loop plus a reviewer pass on every module a trace touches. Interweaving reopens derivations the editor pass closed; that is the accepted cost of the chosen shape.

What Module 0 taught, in one line

Module 0 found four errors in itself. Each one was well-formed, internally consistent, passed all thirteen automated gates, and was wrong: a stiffness ladder calibrated from the moduli it claimed to predict; collagen's compliance attributed to entropy when entropy is short by 642×; a 108 MPa ceiling evaluated outside the hypothesis of its own proposition; and a “half the drop in 12 s” that drawing the figure put at 16 s.

The claim that fails is not the one you compute. It is the one you assert while computing something adjacent.
